# Five-minute pitch transcript

Learning platforms often optimize what gets clicked, not what responsibly helps a learner. LearnBridge asks a different question: can a small recommender be relevant while respecting a learner's knowledge gaps, time, budget, accessibility needs, and exposure to varied resources?

Our prototype starts cold. No click history is required. A learner states a goal, level, weak topics, preferred formats, weekly time, budget, caption needs, and appetite for exploration. The first baseline ranks global quality and popularity, exposing its tendency to show similar items to everyone. The second uses TF-IDF and cosine similarity to personalize retrieval from profile text. The third begins with those candidates, rejects hard constraint violations, scores knowledge-gap coverage, quality, and accessibility, then uses maximal marginal relevance and a provider cap to build a varied top five.

The dashboard makes this inspectable. Every item states its metadata-grounded reason, gap addressed, and responsible-selection note. A comparison table holds the profile constant across systems. Offline evaluation computes relevance, diversity, coverage, constraint, long-tail, and exposure metrics from deterministic synthetic judgments. The lambda chart reveals the real trade-off: more exploration can broaden a list while giving up some pure relevance.

The optional language-model adaptation is deliberately downstream. Qwen 2.5 0.5B Instruct can be LoRA-tuned to rank a tiny candidate set and explain choices while rejecting violations. It never replaces retrieval. The deployed app uses safe templates unless an adapter is explicitly enabled. We do not claim improvement until both base and adapted outputs are actually evaluated; the repository keeps missing results clearly labeled, not fabricated.

This is an educational prototype. The catalog and judgments are synthetic, provider quality is not externally verified, and the system does not replace educators or advisors. The next step is educator-reviewed relevance data, real accessibility audits, subgroup fairness analysis, and outcome monitoring. LearnBridge shows that responsible recommendation need not be mysterious or infrastructure-heavy: constraints can be explicit, trade-offs can be measured, and every recommendation can be accountable to the data that supports it.
